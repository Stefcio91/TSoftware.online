Before you startHow to use this list

How to use this list

Thirty cards. Each one is a repetitive job that somebody in a typical company still does by hand today, and a way to take it off people's plates in a few days.

01

Pick one process

Don't read this like a catalogue. Skim the list and mark three things your company does by hand every day. Then pick one. One, not five.

02

Work out what it costs

Every process has three numbers: how many minutes it takes, how many times a day it repeats, and how many days a month. Multiply them. The result usually comes as a surprise, which is why there's a calculator next to this.

03

Start with the one that hurts most

Not the easiest one and not the flashiest. The one that makes somebody in the company roll their eyes. Your team will love that automation within a week, and you'll see that this works.

Sales and quotes

Here every lost hour is a quote that never went out or an enquiry left unanswered. We start where the money is.

01

Orders from email straight into the ERP

What hurtsA customer sends an order by email: a PDF, a spreadsheet, sometimes a photo. Somebody retypes it by hand into Subiekt or Comarch and every so often gets the quantities wrong.

How we automate itAI reads the attachment, pulls out the items, codes and quantities, and matches them to your product records. The order lands in the ERP as a document awaiting approval. The sales rep just checks it and clicks "OK".

How we automate itThe sales rep enters the items in a simple form or types them into a chat. The system pulls current prices and the customer's discounts, builds a PDF from a template and sends it. A copy goes to the CRM.

Where to startFour steps and one rule

Where to start

You don't have to pick all thirty. One process and one conversation is enough. This is what it looks like from the first email to a running automation.

  1. 1

    Let's talk

    Half an hour on a call. You tell me what hurts. I ask who does it, how many times a day and where the data lives. No slides and no commitments.

    30 min · online or on site
  2. 2

    Design and quote

    You get a diagram of the automation, a list of the systems we'll connect, and a single price. Next to it: how many hours a month you save and when the cost pays for itself.

    1–2 working days
  3. 3

    We build and test

    Usually 2 to 7 days. The automation first runs on test data, then alongside your current process. You check the results, I fix what needs fixing.

    2–7 days · a week
  4. 4

    We train and keep watch

    I show the team how to use it and what to do if something breaks. For the first month I watch the logs and tighten whatever needs it.

    1 month of care included
Rule number one

We count the savings before you pay.

Before we build anything, we work out together how many hours and how much money the automation will save: minutes × times a day × days, just like on page 2. That number goes into the quote next to the price.

If the maths doesn't add up, I say so plainly and we don't take it on. An automation that doesn't pay for itself within a few months is a bad automation.

    Even roughly. "Anna, about 2 hours a day" is a perfectly good answer.
